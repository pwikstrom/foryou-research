A quick look inside the Data Hub

This is a little peek at the Hub's analysis tools, running against a sample dataset we've made available to you. It's not the full pipeline — just enough to get a feel for what the Data Hub makes possible.

What you can explore here is a sample dataset we've opened up for a look around. Ingesting your own participants' donations and running the full ingest-to-insight pipeline isn't part of this preview — when you're ready for that, contact us for a demo.

Getting access

  1. Create an account. Go to the sign-up page and register with your email address, a username and a password.
  2. Wait for approval. New accounts are reviewed before they're enabled for the sample dataset. You'll see "pending approval" until your account is activated — if it takes more than a few days, email foryou@qut.edu.au.
  3. Log in. You'll land in the app with the analysis tabs — Explore, Timelines, Video Analysis, Correlations and Semantic Space — ready to use against the sample study. Ingestion and administration aren't part of this peek.

Take the analysis tools for a spin

With the sample study loaded, here's a quick tour of what you can do:

  1. Pick the sample study. Everything in the hub operates on the active study — a defined dataset combining collections and a date range. Choose the sample study from the dropdown in the header; the studies you can access are listed under My stuff → My Studies.
  2. Get an overview in Explore. Open the Explore tab to see how any variable is distributed across the study. Use the filter panel to slice the data — for example, videos from a particular content category, or plays longer than a threshold — and switch to two-slice mode to compare groups side by side. Significance stars flag differences between the slices.
  3. Follow a participant in Timelines. Pick a collection (one participant's donation) and watch how their feed composition evolves day by day. Filter chips isolate rising, falling, spiking or volatile categories, and "Show Findings" lists detected trends, anomalies and structural breaks.
  4. Drill into videos. From any chart in Explore or Correlations, click through to Video Analysis to watch the underlying videos, inspect their full metadata and AI annotations, and add your own tags and notes.
  5. Go deeper. Correlations charts relationships between numeric measures (with regression lines, group ellipses and a correlation heatmap), and Semantic Space maps every annotated video by meaning so you can explore the corpus's micro-genres.

Tips

  • Every tab has a ? help button in the top right with a detailed description of that tab.
  • Customize which variables appear on each surface via the ⚙ panels — your choices are personal and persist across sessions.
  • Charts stay synchronised when you zoom or pan in Timelines; click any point for that day's stats.
  • Other researchers' annotations appear in Video Analysis when they've opted into sharing.
